1. Learning objectives

By the end of this lecture you should be able to:

  1. Define digital citizenship and explain digital identity and digital footprint.
  2. Apply netiquette in emails, chats, comments and online classes.
  3. Recognise cyberbullying and harassment, and know how to respond and report.
  4. Configure privacy settings and create strong passwords with two-step verification.
  5. Describe the main provisions of PECA 2016 relevant to students.

Relevant CLOs: CLO 4 – Understand ethical and legal aspects of ICT.

2. Introduction

Citizenship means having rights and responsibilities in a community. The internet is now a community we all live in, and digital citizenship means behaving in it safely, respectfully and responsibly. For students, the stakes are real: a photo posted at sixteen can be found by an employer at twenty-five, a cruel comment can deeply hurt a classmate, and a weak password can let someone take over your social media account. This lecture moves from the tools of earlier units to the people who use them.

Roman Urdu: Internet par jo kuch hum post karte hain woh hamara digital footprint ban jata hai aur aksar hamesha ke liye reh jata hai. Achha digital citizen izzat se baat karta hai, cyberbullying ki report karta hai, privacy settings sahi rakhta hai aur mazboot password aur two-step verification istemal karta hai.

3. Detailed explanation

3.1 Digital identity and digital footprint

Your digital identity is how you appear online: your names, photos, profiles, posts and the groups you join. Your digital footprint is the trail of data you leave. An active footprint is what you deliberately share, such as posts, comments and photos. A passive footprint is collected without you actively posting, such as location history, search history, cookies and app usage. Footprints are persistent (screenshots and archives last), searchable and can be seen by audiences you did not intend, such as future employers, universities and scholarship committees. Search your own name regularly to see what others can find.

Key points
  • Digital identity: how you appear online
  • Active footprint: posts, comments, photos
  • Passive footprint: location, searches, cookies
  • Persistent, searchable, wide audience

3.2 Online reputation and netiquette

Online reputation is what others think of you based on your digital footprint. Netiquette (network etiquette) is the set of polite behaviours expected online. Core rules: remember there is a human on the other side; write as you would speak face to face; avoid all capitals, which reads as shouting; respect others’ privacy by not sharing their photos or numbers without permission; check facts before forwarding; keep group chats on topic; respect time zones and working hours when messaging teachers; disagree with ideas, not people. In online classes, use your real name, mute when not speaking and use the chat appropriately.

Key points
  • Think before you post
  • No ALL CAPS, slurs or insults
  • Ask before sharing others’ photos
  • Verify before forwarding
  • Disagree respectfully

3.3 Cyberbullying and online harassment

Cyberbullying is using digital technology to repeatedly threaten, embarrass, insult or exclude someone. Forms include abusive messages, spreading rumours, creating fake profiles, sharing private photos, excluding someone from groups and online stalking. Harassment may also be sexual or blackmail-based. Victims often feel anxious and isolated because the abuse follows them home on their phones. The response steps are: do not reply in anger; take screenshots as evidence with dates; block the person; use the platform’s report tool; tell a trusted adult, teacher or counsellor; and for serious threats or blackmail report to the FIA cybercrime wing. Bystanders should support the victim and report, not share the content.

Key points
  • Forms: abuse, rumours, fake profiles, exclusion, stalking
  • Do not retaliate
  • Save evidence, block, report
  • Tell a trusted adult; FIA for serious cases
  • Bystanders: support and report

3.4 Privacy settings and account security

Privacy settings control who can see your posts, profile details, friends list and contact information. On Facebook and Instagram, set the audience to Friends or a private account, limit who can find you by phone number, review tagged photos before they appear and turn off location on posts. On WhatsApp, limit who sees profile photo, last seen and about, and who can add you to groups. A strong password is long (at least 12 characters), unique for each important account and not based on personal information; a passphrase of four random words with a number and symbol is easy to remember. Two-step verification (2SV) adds a second check, usually a code from an app or SMS, so a stolen password alone is not enough. A password manager can store unique passwords safely.

Key points
  • Audience: Friends / private
  • Limit phone-number search and group adds
  • Review tags; disable post location
  • Strong, unique passwords or passphrases
  • Turn on two-step verification

3.5 Laws and responsibilities: PECA 2016

Pakistan’s Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act 2016 (PECA) defines cybercrimes and penalties. Offences relevant to students include unauthorised access to someone’s account or data, identity misuse, cyberstalking, spreading sexually explicit images of a person without consent, and spoofing and fraud. The FIA’s cybercrime wing investigates complaints. The law has been amended since its introduction and some provisions are debated, so check current official sources for details. Beyond the law, digital citizens have responsibilities: respect intellectual property, protect others’ privacy and contribute positively, for example by sharing accurate information and helping others learn.

Key points
  • PECA 2016: cybercrime law of Pakistan
  • Unauthorised access and identity misuse are offences
  • Cyberstalking and sharing private images are offences
  • Complaints to FIA cybercrime wing
  • Check current official text; law has been amended

4. Technical terminology

Digital citizenship
Responsible, safe and respectful use of technology.
Digital footprint
The trail of data left by online activity.
Netiquette
Polite behaviour rules for online communication.
Cyberbullying
Repeated harmful behaviour using digital technology.
Two-step verification
A second check after the password, such as a code.
Passphrase
A long password made of several words.
Privacy settings
Controls deciding who can see your information.
PECA
Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act 2016 of Pakistan.

Important definitions

Digital identity: The collection of information about a person that exists online.
Online reputation: The perception others form about a person or organisation from online information.
Cyber harassment: Using electronic communication to intimidate, threaten or distress another person.

5. Step-by-step example

Worked example: securing a social media account

  1. Open Settings → Accounts Centre → Password and security.
  2. Change the password to a unique passphrase such as Baghsar-Lake-Mango-27!
  3. Turn on two-factor authentication using an authenticator app or SMS.
  4. Save the backup codes in a safe place offline.
  5. Check ‘Where you’re logged in’ and log out of unknown devices.
  6. Open Privacy settings: set future posts to Friends and limit phone-number lookup.
  7. Turn on review for tags and disable location on posts.

6. Pakistan-based examples

FIA cybercrime reporting

The FIA cybercrime wing accepts complaints about online harassment, fraud and account hacking through its official channels, and has offices in major cities.

Digital Rights Foundation

This Pakistani non-profit runs a cyber harassment helpline that offers guidance to people facing online abuse.

7. Bhimber case study

Case study: a fake profile targeting a student

A Class 10 student in Bhimber discovers a fake Instagram account using her photos and posting insulting content. Her friends start forwarding screenshots. Her ICT teacher helps her respond: she does not message the fake account, takes dated screenshots, reports the account to Instagram as impersonation, asks friends to report rather than share, informs her parents and the school, and switches her own account to private. The fake account is removed. The school then runs a session on netiquette and bystander responsibility.

Questions for analysis
  1. Which response steps did she follow?
  2. What should the friends have done instead of forwarding?
  3. Which privacy settings reduce the chance of this happening?

8. Real-life applications

  • Managing personal social media safely
  • Respectful participation in class groups and forums
  • Protecting family accounts and phones
  • Building a positive professional reputation
  • Supporting peers facing online abuse

9. Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Positive online presence can open academic and job opportunities
  • Respectful online communities support learning
  • Strong security prevents account takeover
  • Awareness reduces harm from bullying and scams

Disadvantages

  • Digital footprints are hard to erase
  • Cyberbullying can be anonymous and constant
  • Privacy settings change and can be confusing
  • Victims may hesitate to report

  • Define digital footprint and give active and passive examples.
  • List at least six netiquette rules.

13. Lecture summary

  • Digital citizenship is safe, respectful and responsible technology use.
  • Footprints are persistent; think before posting.
  • Netiquette keeps online communication respectful.
  • Respond to cyberbullying by saving evidence, blocking, reporting and telling someone.
  • Use privacy settings, strong unique passwords and two-step verification; know PECA 2016.

16. Assignment

Conduct a digital footprint audit: search your name, review your social media profiles and list what is publicly visible. Then write a two-page report on changes you made (privacy settings, removed posts, 2SV enabled) and create a one-page netiquette poster for your class WhatsApp or LMS group.

Homework

Turn on two-step verification for your main email account and write down where you stored the backup codes (not the codes themselves).

17. Suggested classroom discussion

  1. Should schools punish students for online behaviour outside school?
  2. Is anonymous posting good or bad for society?
  3. How can parents support children online without invading privacy?
