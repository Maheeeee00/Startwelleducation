1. Learning objectives

By the end of this lecture you should be able to:

  1. Explain how fitness and health tracking apps collect and use data.
  2. Describe telemedicine and its benefits and limits in Pakistan.
  3. Compare mobile wallets, internet banking and instant payment systems.
  4. Shop online safely by evaluating sellers, reviews, return policies and payment options.
  5. Identify common digital fraud methods and protective measures.

Relevant CLOs: CLO 2 – Identify different ICT platforms and tools.; CLO 3 – Apply ICT tools in everyday, academic and professional activities.

2. Introduction

Three areas of daily life have been changed by ICT more than almost any other for ordinary Pakistani families: health, money and shopping. A grandmother in a village can now consult a doctor by video, a student in Bhimber receives money from home in seconds through a mobile wallet, and a family orders school shoes online with cash on delivery. These services bring convenience but also new risks: health data privacy, fraud calls asking for OTPs and fake online sellers. This lecture covers both the use and the safe use of these tools.

Roman Urdu: Aaj phone par doctor se mashwara, bill ki adaigi aur shopping sab mumkin hai. Fitness apps qadam aur neend record karti hain, telemedicine se door baithe doctor mil jata hai, JazzCash aur Easypaisa se paise bheje jaate hain aur Daraz par cheezein mangwai jaati hain. Sab se aham usool: PIN aur OTP kabhi kisi ko na batayein.

3. Detailed explanation

3.1 Fitness and health tracking

Smartphones and wearables such as fitness bands and smartwatches contain sensors: an accelerometer counts steps, a heart-rate sensor measures pulse and GPS records running routes. Apps such as Google Fit, Samsung Health and the Mi Fitness (Zepp) app for Mi Band store this data, show trends and set goals such as 8,000 steps a day. Some devices estimate sleep quality and blood oxygen. These numbers are estimates, useful for motivation and habits but not medical diagnoses. Health data is sensitive personal information, so review which apps can access it and whether it is shared with third parties.

Key points
  • Sensors: accelerometer, heart rate, GPS
  • Apps: Google Fit, Samsung Health, Mi Fitness
  • Goals, trends, reminders
  • Estimates, not diagnoses; protect health data

3.2 Telemedicine and digital health

Telemedicine delivers healthcare at a distance through video, audio or chat. In Pakistan, Oladoc and Marham let patients search for doctors by speciality and city, read profiles, book in-clinic or online appointments and pay online. Sehat Kahani connects patients, especially in underserved areas, with female doctors through e-clinics and an app. Benefits include saving travel time and cost, access to specialists from small towns and follow-up consultations from home. Limits include the lack of physical examination, the need for stable internet and the risk of unverified ‘doctors’ on social media. Always check that a doctor is registered with the Pakistan Medical and Dental Council (PM&DC).

Key points
  • Online booking and video consultation
  • Oladoc, Marham, Sehat Kahani
  • Saves travel; reaches remote areas
  • No physical examination; verify registration

3.3 Digital finance: wallets, banking and payments

Mobile wallets such as JazzCash and Easypaisa let users send and receive money, pay utility bills, buy mobile load and pay merchants by QR code, using only a phone number and CNIC-verified account. Internet and mobile banking apps from banks let customers transfer funds, pay bills and check statements. Raast, Pakistan’s instant payment system run by the State Bank of Pakistan, allows free, instant transfers between banks and wallets using a Raast ID, usually the mobile number. Security relies on a PIN, OTPs sent by SMS and device binding. Banks and wallets never ask customers to share their PIN, OTP or full card details by call, SMS or email.

Key points
  • Wallets: JazzCash, Easypaisa
  • Mobile and internet banking
  • Raast: instant interbank transfers using mobile number
  • PIN, OTP, biometric verification

3.4 E-commerce and safe online shopping

E-commerce platforms such as Daraz, Telemart and Shophive sell products from many sellers or their own stock and deliver across Pakistan. Before buying, compare prices, read recent reviews with photos, check the seller’s rating and number of orders, and read the return and warranty policy. Payment options include cash on delivery (COD), cards, wallets and bank transfer. COD reduces the risk of paying for an item that never arrives. Be careful with products advertised only on social media pages that demand advance payment, prices far below market and pressure such as ‘only 2 left’.

Key points
  • Compare prices and read recent reviews
  • Check seller rating and return policy
  • Payment: COD, card, wallet, bank transfer
  • Avoid advance payment to unknown pages

3.5 Digital fraud and protection

Common frauds in Pakistan include calls pretending to be from a bank, wallet or the Benazir Income Support Programme asking for OTPs; fake prize messages; SIM swap attempts; phishing links that copy bank login pages; and fake online shops. Protection rules: never share PIN, OTP or CVV; check the website address and padlock before entering details; enable app locks and biometric login; set SMS alerts for every transaction; use official apps from the Play Store or App Store; and report fraud immediately to your bank’s helpline and block the account or card. Cybercrime can be reported to the FIA’s cybercrime wing.

Key points
  • Never share PIN, OTP, CVV
  • Check URL and official app
  • Transaction alerts and app locks
  • Report to bank helpline and FIA cybercrime

4. Technical terminology

Wearable
A smart device worn on the body, such as a fitness band.
Telemedicine
Remote medical consultation using ICT.
Mobile wallet
An app that stores money and makes payments using a phone.
OTP
One-Time Password sent for verifying a transaction.
Raast
Pakistan’s instant payment system run by the State Bank of Pakistan.
QR code
A two-dimensional barcode used here for payments.
COD
Cash on delivery: paying when the item is received.
SIM swap
Fraud in which a criminal obtains a duplicate of your SIM to receive your OTPs.

Important definitions

E-health: The use of ICT to deliver health services and information.
Digital financial services: Financial services such as payments, transfers and banking delivered through digital channels.
E-commerce: Buying and selling goods and services over the internet.

5. Step-by-step example

Worked example: checking a Daraz product before buying

  1. Search the product and sort by ‘Best match’ or ratings.
  2. Open the listing and compare the price with two other sellers.
  3. Check the seller’s positive rating percentage and how long they have been selling.
  4. Read recent reviews, especially those with customer photos.
  5. Check the warranty and return policy (for example 7 or 14 days).
  6. Choose cash on delivery or a secure wallet payment.
  7. On delivery, check the package before paying if COD allows, and keep the receipt.

6. Pakistan-based examples

Raast adoption

Since its launch, Raast has made free instant person-to-person transfers between banks and wallets common, using a mobile number as the Raast ID.

Sehat Kahani

This Pakistani health platform links patients in remote and low-income areas with qualified female doctors through e-clinics and a mobile app.

7. Bhimber case study

Case study: a fraud call in Bhimber

A shopkeeper in Bhimber receives a call: ‘This is Easypaisa head office, your account will be blocked. Tell me the code we just sent to verify.’ The code is actually an OTP for a transfer from his wallet. His son, who has studied ICT, stops him, ends the call, calls the official helpline number printed in the app and confirms the account is safe. The family then turns on transaction alerts, sets an app PIN different from the SIM PIN and teaches the shop staff never to share codes.

Questions for analysis
  1. Which signs showed this was fraud?
  2. What would have happened if the OTP was shared?
  3. List three protective steps the family took.

8. Real-life applications

  • Booking doctors and online consultations
  • Tracking fitness goals
  • Paying bills, fees and sending money home
  • Online shopping with delivery
  • Merchant payments by QR code in shops

9. Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Saves time and travel
  • Access to doctors and products from small cities
  • Instant, low-cost money transfers
  • Transaction records improve transparency

Disadvantages

  • Fraud and scams targeting less-aware users
  • Health and financial data privacy risks
  • Needs internet and smartphones
  • Fake products and sellers online

12. Important points for examination

  • Give two Pakistani examples each for telemedicine, wallets and e-commerce.
  • List at least five fraud protection measures.
  • Explain what OTP is and why it must never be shared.
  • Discuss advantages and disadvantages of ICT in health or finance.

13. Lecture summary

  • Fitness apps use sensors to track health habits, not to diagnose.
  • Telemedicine connects patients to doctors remotely; verify registration.
  • Wallets, mobile banking and Raast make payments instant.
  • Shop safely by checking sellers, reviews and policies; COD reduces risk.
  • Never share PIN or OTP; use official apps and report fraud quickly.

16. Assignment

Prepare a ‘Digital Safety Guide for My Family’ (3 pages) covering: two telemedicine services and how to verify a doctor, how to use one mobile wallet safely, five rules for online shopping, five common frauds in Pakistan and what to do if money is stolen.
