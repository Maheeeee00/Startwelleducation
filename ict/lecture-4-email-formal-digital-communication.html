1. Learning objectives

By the end of this lecture you should be able to:

  1. Explain how email is sent and received and identify parts of an email address.
  2. Use To, CC, BCC, attachments, signatures and folders correctly.
  3. Write a formal email with a clear subject, greeting, body and closing.
  4. Apply email and meeting etiquette.
  5. Schedule and conduct a video meeting on Google Meet, Teams or Zoom.

Relevant CLOs: CLO 2 – Identify different ICT platforms and tools.; CLO 3 – Apply ICT tools in everyday, academic and professional activities.

2. Introduction

WhatsApp is perfect for friends, but universities, employers and government offices still communicate formally by email. Your first contact with a teacher about a missed quiz, an application for an internship or a complaint to a department will usually be an email, and its tone will shape how people see you. Video conferencing has also become part of normal study and work since online classes became common. This lecture teaches the tools and, more importantly, the manners of professional digital communication.

Roman Urdu: Email aaj bhi taleem aur dafatir mein rasmi rabtay ka sab se aham zariya hai. To, CC aur BCC ka sahi istemal, saaf subject line, izzat bhara lehja aur sahi attachment ek achi email ki pehchan hain. Video meetings mein mic, camera aur waqt ki pabandi zaroori hai.

3. Detailed explanation

3.1 How email works

An email address has two parts separated by @: the user name and the domain, for example ali.khan@gmail.com or registrar@uob.edu.pk. When you click Send, your email client (Gmail, Outlook) passes the message to your provider’s outgoing mail server using the SMTP protocol. That server finds the recipient’s mail server from the domain name and delivers it. The recipient’s app then downloads or displays it using IMAP or POP3. Email is asynchronous: the receiver does not need to be online at the same moment, which makes it ideal for formal records.

Key points
  • Address = username @ domain
  • SMTP sends mail; IMAP/POP3 receive it
  • Webmail (Gmail in browser) vs client (Outlook app)
  • Asynchronous: a permanent written record

3.2 To, CC, BCC and attachments

To is for the main person who must read and act on the email. CC (carbon copy) is for people who should be kept informed; everyone can see who was CC’d. BCC (blind carbon copy) hides recipients from each other, which protects privacy when emailing a large group, such as a teacher writing to all parents. Attachments add files; keep them small (Gmail limits attachments to 25 MB and offers a Drive link for larger files), name them clearly such as Assignment2_AliKhan.pdf and prefer PDF so formatting does not change. Reply answers only the sender; Reply all answers everyone in To and CC, so use it carefully. Forward sends the message to someone new.

Key points
  • To: main recipient who must act
  • CC: informed, visible to all
  • BCC: hidden recipients, protects privacy
  • Reply vs Reply all vs Forward
  • Attachments: clear names, PDF, under 25 MB

3.3 Structure of a formal email

A formal email has six parts. Subject line: short and specific, such as ‘Request for leave on 12 March – BSCS Section A’. Greeting: ‘Dear Sir/Madam’, ‘Dear Dr. Ahmed’ or ‘Respected Ma’am’. Opening line: who you are and why you are writing. Body: the details in short paragraphs, with dates and reference numbers. Closing: a polite request or thanks, such as ‘I would be grateful for your consideration.’ Signature: full name, roll number or designation, department and contact number. Avoid SMS language, all capital letters, emojis and slang in formal emails.

Key points
  • Subject
  • Greeting
  • Opening: identity and purpose
  • Body: details in short paragraphs
  • Closing request or thanks
  • Signature: name, roll no., department, phone

3.4 Email etiquette and safety

Reply within one or two working days, proofread before sending and check that attachments are actually attached. Do not forward chain messages. Use a professional address, ideally firstname.lastname rather than a nickname. Organise your inbox with labels or folders and archive old messages. Be alert to phishing: emails that pretend to be from a bank, university or courier and ask you to click a link or share a password or OTP. Check the sender’s real address, hover over links before clicking and never share a PIN or OTP by email.

Key points
  • Proofread; check attachments
  • Professional address and tone
  • Labels, folders, archive
  • Recognise phishing: urgency, odd sender, suspicious links

3.5 Video conferencing

Video meetings are synchronous: everyone joins at the same time. Google Meet works in a browser with a Google account and integrates with Google Calendar and Classroom. Microsoft Teams combines chat, meetings and file sharing and is common in universities that use Microsoft 365. Zoom is widely used for webinars and large classes. All three support screen sharing, chat, raising a hand, recording (with permission) and breakout rooms in some plans. Good meeting etiquette: join on time, keep the microphone muted when not speaking, use a real name, turn the camera on when asked, use the chat for questions and stay focused.

Key points
  • Google Meet: browser, Calendar and Classroom links
  • Microsoft Teams: chat + meetings + files
  • Zoom: webinars and large classes
  • Etiquette: mute, real name, camera, chat, punctuality

4. Technical terminology

SMTP
Simple Mail Transfer Protocol, used to send email.
IMAP
Protocol that keeps email synchronised on the server across devices.
CC
Carbon copy: an additional visible recipient.
BCC
Blind carbon copy: a hidden recipient.
Attachment
A file sent along with an email.
Phishing
A fraudulent message designed to steal passwords or money.
Synchronous
Communication in real time, such as a video call.
Asynchronous
Communication not in real time, such as email.

Important definitions

Email: Electronic mail: a method of exchanging digital messages and files over the internet.
Netiquette in email: Accepted rules of polite and professional behaviour when writing emails.
Video conferencing: Real-time audio and video communication between people in different locations over the internet.

5. Step-by-step example

Worked example: sending a leave application in Gmail

  1. Click Compose.
  2. To: class advisor’s email; CC: department coordinator.
  3. Subject: Leave application for 14–15 March – Ali Khan, BSCS-1A.
  4. Greeting: Respected Sir,
  5. Body: state the reason, dates and that you will cover missed work.
  6. Closing: I shall be thankful. Signature with name, roll number and phone.
  7. Attach the medical certificate as PDF and check it appears below the message.
  8. Proofread, then click Send. Confirm it appears in the Sent folder.

6. Pakistan-based examples

University communication

Most Pakistani universities issue official email accounts ending in .edu.pk. Admission offices and examination departments expect queries from these accounts with roll numbers in the subject line.

Phishing in Pakistan

Fake messages claiming to be from banks, Benazir Income Support Programme or courier companies ask people to share OTPs. Banks repeatedly warn that they never ask for a PIN or OTP by email, SMS or call.

7. Bhimber case study

Case study: a Bhimber school’s parent communication

The principal of a school in Bhimber emails 300 parents about the annual result day. A teacher first types all addresses in the To field, which would show every parent’s email to everyone. The principal corrects this: the school’s own address goes in To and all parents in BCC. The email has a clear subject, the date and time in bold, a Google Maps link and a polite closing. A follow-up Google Meet session is scheduled for parents abroad, with the link added to a Google Calendar invitation.

Questions for analysis
  1. Why was BCC the right choice?
  2. Write a suitable subject line for this email.
  3. List three etiquette rules the parents should follow in the Meet session.

8. Real-life applications

  • Applications for leave, admission and jobs
  • Submitting assignments to teachers
  • Official announcements to large groups
  • Online classes, interviews and viva sessions
  • Client communication in freelancing

9. Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Creates a permanent, searchable record
  • Fast, free and works across the world
  • Can carry documents and links
  • Video meetings save travel time and cost

Disadvantages

  • Phishing and spam
  • Tone can be misunderstood without face-to-face cues
  • Inbox overload
  • Video calls need stable internet and electricity

12. Important points for examination

  • Memorise the difference between CC and BCC with one example each.
  • Know the six parts of a formal email.
  • Be able to write a complete formal email in an exam answer.
  • Name at least two video conferencing tools and three etiquette rules.

13. Lecture summary

  • Email is asynchronous and leaves a written record.
  • Use To for action, CC for information and BCC for privacy.
  • A formal email has subject, greeting, opening, body, closing and signature.
  • Watch for phishing and never share OTPs or passwords.
  • Meet, Teams and Zoom need good etiquette: mute, real name, punctuality.
