ICT terminology glossary

3-2-1 backup rule
Keep three copies of data on two types of media with one copy off-site. (L8)
Absolute reference
A cell reference fixed with $ signs, such as $H$1. (L6)
Academic integrity
Honesty and responsibility in learning, research and assessment. (L12)
Animation
A visual effect applied to an object on a slide. (L7)
Argument
A value or range supplied inside a function’s brackets. (L6)
Artificial Intelligence
Software that performs tasks needing human-like intelligence. (L2)
Asynchronous
Communication not in real time, such as email. (L4)
Asynchronous learning
Self-paced learning without real-time interaction. (L9)
Attachment
A file sent along with an email. (L4)
Backup
An extra copy of data kept to recover from loss. (L8)
Bandwidth
The amount of data a connection can carry per second, measured in Mbps. (L1)
BCC
Blind carbon copy: a hidden recipient. (L4)
Bibliography
A list of sources cited in a document. (L5)
Blended learning
A mix of face-to-face and online learning. (L9)
Breakout room
A smaller sub-meeting within a virtual class. (L9)
Caption
A numbered label for a table or figure. (L5)
CC
Carbon copy: an additional visible recipient. (L4)
Cell
The intersection of a row and a column, such as B3. (L6)
Chart
A graphical representation of spreadsheet data. (L6)
Citation
A reference that tells readers where information came from. (L3)
Cloud computing
On-demand delivery of computing resources such as storage, servers and software over the internet. (L2)
Cloud storage
Saving files on a provider’s internet servers instead of your own device. (L1)
COD
Cash on delivery: paying when the item is received. (L10)
Conditional formatting
Formatting that changes automatically based on cell values. (L6)
Copyright
Legal right of creators over their original works. (L12)
CPU
Central Processing Unit; carries out instructions and calculations. (L1)
Crawler
A program that automatically visits web pages by following links. (L3)
Creative Commons
Licences that allow reuse of works under conditions. (L12)
Cyber harassment
Using electronic communication to intimidate, threaten or distress another person. (L11)
Cyberbullying
Repeated harmful behaviour using digital technology. (L11)
Device driver
Software that lets the operating system control a specific device. (L1)
Digital citizenship
Responsible, safe and respectful use of technology. (L11)
Digital ethics
Moral principles guiding the responsible use of digital technology and information. (L12)
Digital financial services
Financial services such as payments, transfers and banking delivered through digital channels. (L10)
Digital footprint
The trail of data left by online activity. (L11)
Digital identity
The collection of information about a person that exists online. (L11)
Digital literacy
The ability to find, evaluate, use and create information using digital technologies. (L9)
Disinformation
False information spread deliberately to deceive. (L12)
Domain
The address of a website, such as hec.gov.pk. (L3)
E-commerce
Buying and selling goods or services over the internet. (L2)
E-governance
Delivery of government services and information through ICT. (L2)
E-health
The use of ICT to deliver health services and information. (L10)
E-learning
Learning delivered or supported by electronic technology. (L9)
Email
Electronic mail: a method of exchanging digital messages and files over the internet. (L4)
Emerging technology
A new technology that is developing quickly and expected to have a large social or economic effect. (L2)
File management
Organising, naming, storing and backing up files so they can be found and protected. (L8)
Fill handle
Small square used to copy formulas to adjacent cells. (L6)
Format Painter
Tool that copies formatting from one place to another. (L5)
Formula
An expression beginning with = that calculates a value. (L6)
Function
A built-in formula such as SUM or IF. (L6)
Gradebook
LMS record of students’ marks. (L9)
Hardware
The physical, tangible components of an ICT system. (L1)
Header/Footer
Area at the top or bottom of every page. (L5)
ICT
The set of technologies used to create, store, process, transmit and receive information, including computers, software, networks and telecommunication. (L1)
IMAP
Protocol that keeps email synchronised on the server across devices. (L4)
Index
The search engine’s database of words and the pages that contain them. (L3)
Intellectual property
Legally protected creations of the mind. (L12)
IoT
Internet of Things: everyday objects connected to the internet. (L2)
Latency
The delay between sending and receiving data over a network. (L2)
Layout
An arrangement of placeholders on a slide. (L7)
Learning Management System
A software application for administering, delivering, tracking and reporting educational courses. (L9)
Link sharing
Sharing a file by sending a URL rather than a copy. (L8)
LMS
Learning Management System for delivering and tracking courses. (L9)
Machine learning
A type of AI in which systems learn patterns from data. (L2)
Misinformation
False or inaccurate information shared without intent to deceive. (L12)
Mobile wallet
An app that stores money and makes payments using a phone. (L10)
MOOC
Massive Open Online Course. (L9)
Naming convention
A consistent rule for naming files. (L8)
Netiquette
Polite behaviour rules for online communication. (L11)
Netiquette in email
Accepted rules of polite and professional behaviour when writing emails. (L4)
Network
Two or more connected devices that share data and resources. (L1)
Notebook
In OneNote/Evernote, a top-level container of notes. (L8)
Off-site
Stored in a different physical location, such as the cloud. (L8)
Online reputation
The perception others form about a person or organisation from online information. (L11)
Operating system
System software that manages hardware, files and applications. (L1)
OTP
One-Time Password sent for verifying a transaction. (L10)
Paraphrase
Restating an idea fully in your own words. (L12)
Passphrase
A long password made of several words. (L11)
PECA
Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act 2016 of Pakistan. (L11)
Peer review
Checking of research by other experts before publication. (L3)
Peripheral
An external device connected to a computer, such as a printer. (L1)
Permission
The level of access given to a shared file: view, comment or edit. (L8)
Phishing
A fraudulent message designed to steal passwords or money. (L4)
Placeholder
A box on a layout that holds text, images or charts. (L7)
Plagiarism
Using someone else’s work without proper credit. (L12)
Presentation software
Application software used to create a sequence of slides for displaying information to an audience. (L7)
Presenter View
A view showing notes, timer and next slide to the presenter. (L7)
Privacy settings
Controls deciding who can see your information. (L11)
Protocol
An agreed set of rules for communication, such as TCP/IP or HTTP. (L1)
Public domain
Works free for anyone to use without permission. (L12)
QR code
A two-dimensional barcode used here for payments. (L10)
Query
The words typed into a search engine. (L3)
Raast
Pakistan’s instant payment system run by the State Bank of Pakistan. (L10)
RAM
Random Access Memory; fast temporary memory that is cleared when power is off. (L1)
Range
A rectangular group of cells, such as A1:C10. (L6)
Rehearsal
Practising a presentation aloud with timing before delivering it. (L7)
Reliable source
A source that is current, written by a qualified author, supported by evidence and published for an informative purpose. (L3)
Reverse image search
Searching with an image to find its original source. (L12)
SaaS
Software as a Service: applications used through a browser on subscription or free. (L2)
Scope of ICT
The range of fields and activities in which ICT is applied. (L2)
Search engine
A software system that finds and ranks web pages that match a user’s query. (L3)
Search operator
A symbol or word that refines a search, such as quotes or site:. (L3)
Section break
A divider that allows different page layouts in one document. (L5)
SERP
Search Engine Results Page. (L3)
SIM swap
Fraud in which a criminal obtains a duplicate of your SIM to receive your OTPs. (L10)
Slide Master
The top-level slide that controls the design of all layouts. (L7)
SMTP
Simple Mail Transfer Protocol, used to send email. (L4)
Software
A set of programs and instructions that direct hardware to perform tasks. (L1)
Speaker notes
Notes for the presenter that the audience does not see. (L7)
Spreadsheet
Application software that organises data in rows and columns and performs calculations using formulas. (L6)
Style
A named set of formatting applied to text, such as Heading 1. (L5)
Sync
Automatically keeping files identical across devices and the cloud. (L8)
Synchronous
Communication in real time, such as a video call. (L4)
Synchronous learning
Real-time online learning with live interaction. (L9)
Table of contents
An automatically generated list of headings with page numbers. (L5)
Telemedicine
Providing medical consultation remotely using ICT. (L2)
Template
A pre-designed document used as a starting point. (L5)
Theme
A set of colours, fonts and effects applied to all slides. (L7)
Track Changes
A feature that records edits for review. (L5)
Transition
A visual effect when moving from one slide to the next. (L7)
Two-step verification
A second check after the password, such as a code. (L11)
Version history
Saved earlier versions of a file that can be restored. (L8)
Video conferencing
Real-time audio and video communication between people in different locations over the internet. (L4)
Virtual classroom
An online environment that allows teachers and students to interact in real time. (L9)
Visual hierarchy
Arranging elements so the most important information is noticed first. (L7)
Wearable
A smart device worn on the body, such as a fitness band. (L10)
Web research
The systematic process of finding, evaluating and using information from the internet. (L3)
Word processor
Application software used to create, edit, format and print text documents. (L5)
Workbook
A spreadsheet file containing one or more worksheets. (L6)

Student learning guides

How to study this course

Read the lecture notes before class, attend the lecture, complete the lab the same week, attempt the quiz and mark the lecture complete on the dashboard.

Before each lab

Read the lab’s background theory and procedure, make sure you can log in to Google/Microsoft accounts, and create your ICT-Labs cloud folder.

Exam preparation

Use the ‘Important points for examination’ and summary of each lecture, practise the MCQs and short questions on the Assessments page and repeat practical tasks without notes.

If you do not have a computer at home

Use Google Docs, Sheets and Slides on your phone, download materials on campus Wi-Fi, and use lab hours for practical tasks.
