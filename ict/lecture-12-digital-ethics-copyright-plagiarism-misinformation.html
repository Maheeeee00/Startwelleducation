1. Learning objectives

By the end of this lecture you should be able to:

  1. Explain intellectual property, copyright, fair use and Creative Commons licences.
  2. Define plagiarism and avoid it through quoting, paraphrasing, summarising and citing.
  3. Use AI tools in line with academic integrity rules.
  4. Identify misinformation and disinformation using verification steps.
  5. Plan and complete the Digital Portfolio final project.

Relevant CLOs: CLO 3 – Apply ICT tools in everyday, academic and professional activities.; CLO 4 – Understand ethical and legal aspects of ICT.

2. Introduction

Throughout this course you have created documents, spreadsheets, presentations and online content, and you have used material made by others. This final lecture asks ethical questions: who owns digital work, when you may use it, how to give credit and how to avoid spreading false information. These questions matter more each year, as copying is just a right-click away and AI tools can produce text in seconds. We end by launching the Digital Portfolio, which brings together every skill from the course.

Roman Urdu: Kisi aur ka kaam bina ijazat ya bina hawale ke istemal karna ghalat hai. Copyright kaam ke maalik ko haq deta hai; Creative Commons kuch sharton ke saath istemal ki ijazat deta hai. Plagiarism se bachne ke liye citation aur apne alfaaz mein paraphrase karein. Fake news pehchanne ke liye source, date aur saboot check karein. Aakhir mein final project: Digital Portfolio.

3. Detailed explanation

3.1 Intellectual property and copyright

Intellectual property (IP) is creative work of the mind that the law protects, including writing, music, images, software, inventions and brand names. Copyright protects original creative works such as books, articles, photos, videos, music and code from the moment they are created; the owner has the right to copy, distribute, adapt and display the work. In Pakistan, copyright is governed by the Copyright Ordinance 1962 and IP matters are administered by the Intellectual Property Organisation of Pakistan (IPO-Pakistan). Other IP types include trademarks (brand names and logos) and patents (inventions). Finding an image on Google does not mean it is free to use.

Key points
  • IP: creations of the mind
  • Copyright: books, images, music, video, software
  • Trademark: brand names and logos
  • Patent: inventions
  • ‘Available online’ ≠ ‘free to use’

3.2 Fair use and Creative Commons

Many copyright laws allow limited use of works without permission for purposes such as criticism, commentary, news reporting, teaching and research, often called fair use or fair dealing. It depends on the purpose, the amount used and the effect on the original’s market, and it is not a blanket permission. Creative Commons (CC) licences let creators give permission in advance. CC BY allows use with credit; NC means non-commercial only; ND means no changes; SA means share adapted works under the same licence. CC0 places work in the public domain. Sources of openly licensed media include Wikimedia Commons, Unsplash and Pexels (check each image’s licence) and Google Images’ ‘Usage rights → Creative Commons licences’ filter.

Key points
  • Fair use / fair dealing: limited, purpose-based
  • CC BY · NC · ND · SA · CC0
  • Always credit: title, author, source, licence
  • Open media: Wikimedia Commons, Unsplash, Pexels

3.3 Plagiarism and how to avoid it

Plagiarism is presenting someone else’s words, ideas, images or code as your own without proper credit. It includes copying and pasting text, paraphrasing without citation, buying or borrowing assignments, submitting the same work for two courses without permission (self-plagiarism) and copying code. Pakistani universities follow HEC’s plagiarism policy and commonly check work with similarity tools such as Turnitin; penalties range from grade reduction to cancellation of a degree. To avoid plagiarism: quote exact words in quotation marks with a citation; paraphrase by rewriting ideas fully in your own words and structure, still with a citation; summarise main points briefly with a citation; keep notes of sources while researching; and use a consistent citation style such as APA.

Key points
  • Types: copy-paste, uncited paraphrase, buying work, self-plagiarism, code copying
  • Quote + cite
  • Paraphrase in your own words + cite
  • Summarise + cite
  • Reference list in APA/IEEE

3.4 AI tools and academic integrity

AI writing and image tools can explain concepts, suggest outlines and check grammar, but they can also produce invented facts and references, and submitting AI-generated work as your own may break your institution’s rules. Good practice: check your teacher’s and university’s policy for each assignment; use AI to learn, not to replace your own thinking; verify every fact and reference in an original source; disclose AI assistance when required, for example ‘Grammar checked with an AI tool’; and never enter personal or confidential data into public AI tools. Your own understanding is what will be tested in exams, vivas and jobs.

Key points
  • Follow the course AI policy
  • Verify facts and references
  • Disclose AI use when required
  • Protect personal data
  • Learn, do not outsource thinking

3.5 Misinformation and fact-checking

Misinformation is false or misleading information shared without intent to deceive; disinformation is false information spread deliberately; malinformation is true information shared to cause harm. Fake news spreads fast through WhatsApp forwards and social media because it is emotional and surprising. Verify before sharing using the SIFT approach: Stop and check your reaction; Investigate the source; Find better coverage from trusted outlets; Trace claims, quotes and images to the original context. Practical tools include Google reverse image search or Google Lens to find where a photo first appeared, checking the date and checking whether official sources confirm the claim. Be especially careful with health advice, exam leaks and emergency rumours.

Key points
  • Misinformation · Disinformation · Malinformation
  • SIFT: Stop, Investigate, Find, Trace
  • Reverse image search
  • Check date and official confirmation
  • Do not forward unverified messages

4. Technical terminology

Intellectual property
Legally protected creations of the mind.
Copyright
Legal right of creators over their original works.
Creative Commons
Licences that allow reuse of works under conditions.
Public domain
Works free for anyone to use without permission.
Plagiarism
Using someone else’s work without proper credit.
Paraphrase
Restating an idea fully in your own words.
Disinformation
False information spread deliberately to deceive.
Reverse image search
Searching with an image to find its original source.

Important definitions

Digital ethics: Moral principles guiding the responsible use of digital technology and information.
Academic integrity: Honesty and responsibility in learning, research and assessment.
Misinformation: False or inaccurate information shared without intent to deceive.

5. Step-by-step example

Worked example: paraphrasing correctly

  1. Original: ‘Cloud storage allows users to access their files from any device connected to the internet.’ (Author, 2024).
  2. Plagiarised version: ‘Cloud storage lets users access their files from any internet-connected device.’ (only words swapped, no citation).
  3. Read the original, close it and think about the idea.
  4. Write in your own structure: ‘Because files are kept on remote servers, a student can open the same assignment on a phone or a lab computer as long as there is an internet connection.’
  5. Add the citation: (Author, 2024).
  6. Compare with the original to make sure wording and structure are different but meaning is accurate.

6. Pakistan-based examples

HEC plagiarism policy

Higher Education Commission policy requires universities to check theses and research for similarity and sets penalties for plagiarism, making citation skills essential for every student.

Exam paper leaks and fake notices

Every exam season, fake ‘leaked papers’ and forged notifications about date sheets circulate on WhatsApp. The only reliable source is the official board or university website and notice board.

7. Bhimber case study

Case study: a viral forward in Bhimber

A WhatsApp message spreads in Bhimber claiming that schools will close for two weeks due to weather, with a logo of the education department. Students forward it widely. One student applies SIFT: she notices no date or reference number, the logo is blurry, and searching the education department’s official website and Facebook page shows no such notice. A reverse search on the image shows it is an edited notice from a previous year. She posts the official clarification in her class group and asks others to stop forwarding.

Questions for analysis
  1. Which SIFT steps did she use?
  2. Is this misinformation or disinformation? Explain.
  3. What damage could this forward have caused?

8. Real-life applications

  • Writing assignments and theses with proper citations
  • Using images legally in presentations
  • Publishing content on social media responsibly
  • Checking news before sharing in family groups
  • Using AI tools within academic rules

9. Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Respecting IP encourages creativity and fair reward
  • Citation builds credibility and shows research
  • Creative Commons widens legal access to content
  • Fact-checking reduces panic and harm

Disadvantages

  • Copyright rules differ between countries and are complex
  • Similarity tools can flag correctly cited text
  • AI tools make plagiarism easier and harder to detect
  • Misinformation spreads faster than corrections

12. Important points for examination

  • Distinguish copyright, trademark and patent.
  • Explain the four ways to use sources correctly: quote, paraphrase, summarise, cite.
  • Know the meaning of CC BY, NC, ND, SA.
  • Define misinformation and disinformation with examples.

13. Lecture summary

  • Copyright protects creative works; online availability is not permission.
  • Fair use is limited; Creative Commons grants permission with conditions.
  • Avoid plagiarism by quoting, paraphrasing and summarising with citations.
  • Use AI within course rules and verify its output.
  • Use SIFT and reverse image search before sharing; complete the Digital Portfolio.
