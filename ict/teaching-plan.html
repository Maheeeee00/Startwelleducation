Lecture delivery plans

1Lecture 1: ICT Fundamentals: Hardware, Software, Networks and Storage
ItemPlan
Lecture1 – ICT Fundamentals: Hardware, Software, Networks and Storage
Duration50 minutes (one session); 2 theory hours per week
CLO mappingCLO 1
Teaching methodologyInteractive lecture with real objects, think-pair-share and a short sorting activity.
Previous knowledge requiredBasic familiarity with using a phone or computer; no formal knowledge required.

Required teaching materials

  • Projector and laptop
  • An old desktop or opened CPU case (or photos) to show components
  • A phone, USB drive and network cable as real objects
  • Whiteboard and markers

Introduction and motivation

Hold up a phone and ask: ‘How many different computers are inside this?’ Collect guesses, then reveal that it contains a processor, storage, radios and sensors, all parts of one ICT system.

Suggested 50-minute delivery structure

ActivityMinutesWhat happens
Introduction and revisionRevise: none (first lecture). Motivation: Hold up a phone and ask: ‘How many different computers are inside this?’ Collect guesses, then reveal that it contains a processor, storage, radios and sensors, all parts of one ICT system.
Concept explanationWhat is ICT?; Hardware: the physical parts; Software: the instructions; Networks: connecting systems; Storage: local and cloud
Demonstration and examplesOpen File Explorer to show local drives and their capacity, then open Google Drive in a browser to show the same type of file stored in the cloud. Then walk through: Worked example: tracing one WhatsApp photo through an ICT system.
Student participationSorting game: students receive cards with 20 device and software names and place them in the correct column on the board.
Questions and assessmentExit ticket: write one input, one output and one storage device you used today. Ask the classroom questions listed below.
Summary and homeworkRecap the summary points; set homework: Find the storage capacity, RAM and operating system of your phone (Settings → About phone). Calculate roughly how many 3 MB photos could fit in its free space and write your working.
Total50 minutes

Board work

  • ICT = Information + Communication + Technology
  • Data → Processing → Information
  • Input → Process → Output → Storage → Communicate
  • Hardware | Software | Network | Storage (four columns)
  • Local vs Cloud table

Presentation activities

Use the Lecture 1 slides: start with the opening question slide, present the five concept slides during concept explanation, use the worked example and Bhimber case study slides during demonstration, and finish with the recap and closing assessment slides.

Instructor explanation (key points to say)

  • What is ICT?: ICT joins three ideas.
  • Hardware: the physical parts: Hardware is any part of an ICT system you can touch.
  • Software: the instructions: Software is the set of instructions that tells hardware what to do.
  • Networks: connecting systems: A network is two or more devices connected so they can share data and resources such as a printer or an internet connection.
  • Storage: local and cloud: Storage keeps data when the power is switched off.

Student participation

Sorting game: students receive cards with 20 device and software names and place them in the correct column on the board.

Classroom questions

  1. What is the difference between data and information?
  2. Is a printer input or output? Is a touch screen?
  3. Where is a WhatsApp photo stored?

Practical demonstration

Open File Explorer to show local drives and their capacity, then open Google Drive in a browser to show the same type of file stored in the cloud.

Assessment during the lecture

Exit ticket: write one input, one output and one storage device you used today.

Homework

Find the storage capacity, RAM and operating system of your phone (Settings → About phone). Calculate roughly how many 3 MB photos could fit in its free space and write your working.

Learning outcomes achieved

  1. Define ICT and explain each of its three parts: information, communication and technology.
  2. Classify hardware into input, processing, output, storage and communication devices.
  3. Distinguish system software from application software with examples.
  4. Compare LAN, MAN, WAN and the internet by size, ownership and speed.
  5. Compare local storage with cloud storage and choose the right one for a given task.

Use the Lecture 2 slides: start with the opening question slide, present the five concept slides during concept explanation, use the worked example and Bhimber case study slides during demonstration, and finish with the recap and closing assessment slides.

Instructor explanation (key points to say)

  • ICT in education: ICT changes how students learn and how institutions manage teaching.
  • ICT in business: Businesses use ICT to sell, manage and communicate.
  • ICT in governance (e-governance): E-governance means delivering government services and information through ICT.
  • ICT in healthcare and digital media: Hospitals use ICT for patient records, digital imaging, laboratory reports and appointment systems.
  • Emerging technologies: Artificial Intelligence (AI) is software that performs tasks that normally need human intelligence, such as recognising speech, translating languages or recommending videos.

Student participation

Quick poll: match four technologies to four examples.

Learning outcomes achieved

  1. Describe the use of ICT in five sectors: education, business, governance, healthcare and digital media.
  2. Explain AI, IoT, cloud computing and 5G in simple terms.
  3. Give Pakistani examples of e-governance and online banking.
  4. Evaluate one benefit and one risk of each emerging technology.
  5. Propose an ICT plan for a small local business.

  • Choosing keywords: Good searching begins with good keywords.
  • Search operators: Operators are symbols or words that give the search engine exact instructions.
  • Evaluating sources: the CRAAP test: Before using a source, check five things.
  • Google, Bing and specialised tools: Google has the largest share of searches in Pakistan and strong local results.

  3. Use operators: quotes, minus, site:, filetype:, OR and intitle:.
  4. Evaluate a source using the CRAAP test.
  5. Compare Google and Bing and know when to use specialised search tools such as Google Scholar.

Instructor explanation (key points to say)

  • How email works: An email address has two parts separated by @: the user name and the domain, for example ali.khan@gmail.com or registrar@uob.edu.pk.

  • Structure of a formal email: A formal email has six parts.

  • Cell = Column + Row (B4)
  • = start every formula
  • SUM · AVERAGE · MIN · MAX · COUNT · IF

Presentation activities

Use the Lecture 6 slides: start with the opening question slide, present the five concept slides during concept explanation, use the worked example and Bhimber case study slides during demonstration, and finish with the recap and closing assessment slides.

Instructor explanation (key points to say)

  • Spreadsheet basics: A workbook is the whole file; it contains one or more worksheets (tabs).
  • Formulas and operators: Every formula begins with an equals sign.
  • Essential functions: A function is a built-in formula with a name and arguments in brackets.
  • Relative and absolute references: When you copy a formula down a column with the fill handle (the small square at the bottom-right of a selected cell), the cell addresses change relative to the new position: =C2*D2 in row 2 becomes =C3*D3 in row 3.
  • Sorting, filtering, formatting and charts: Sorting rearranges rows by a column, for example highest sales first; always select the whole table so rows stay together.

Student participation

Students predict formula results before pressing Enter; quick ‘find the error’ exercise on a sheet with three deliberate mistakes.

Classroom questions

  1. What will =C2*D2 become in row 5 when copied?
  2. Why does the discount formula break without $?
  3. Which items appear when Status is filtered to Low stock?

Practical demonstration

Build the Bhimber Mart sheet live from the CSV, including the discount cell, IF status, sort, conditional formatting and chart.

Assessment during the lecture

Each student shows a correct IF formula and a chart.

Homework

Create a personal monthly budget sheet with at least eight expense categories, a total, the percentage of each category (=B2/$B$10) and a pie chart.

Learning outcomes achieved

  1. Identify cells, ranges, rows, columns, worksheets and workbooks.
  2. Write formulas with arithmetic operators and the order of operations.
  3. Use SUM, AVERAGE, MIN, MAX, COUNT, COUNTIF and IF functions.
  4. Explain relative and absolute references and use the fill handle.
  5. Sort, filter, apply conditional formatting and create suitable charts.

Presentation activities

Use the Lecture 8 slides: start with the opening question slide, present the five concept slides during concept explanation, use the worked example and Bhimber case study slides during demonstration, and finish with the recap and closing assessment slides.

Instructor explanation (key points to say)

  • Cloud storage services: Google Drive gives 15 GB free shared across Gmail, Drive and Google Photos, and integrates with Docs, Sheets, Slides and Classroom.
  • Sync, version history and offline access: Sync means keeping the same files identical on several devices and the cloud.
  • Sharing and permissions: Cloud sharing sends a link instead of a file copy, so everyone sees the latest version.
  • Folder structure and file naming: A folder structure is a hierarchy that matches how you think about your work, for example University → Semester 1 → ICT → Lectures / Labs / Assignments / Exams.
  • Digital note-taking and backup: Microsoft OneNote organises notes into notebooks, sections and pages, like a ring binder; it accepts typed text, handwriting, images, audio and web clippings, and syncs through OneDrive.

Student participation

Instructor explanation (key points to say)

  • Intellectual property and copyright: Intellectual property (IP) is creative work of the mind that the law protects, including writing, music, images, software, inventions and brand names.
  • Fair use and Creative Commons: Many copyright laws allow limited use of works without permission for purposes such as criticism, commentary, news reporting, teaching and research, often called fair use or fair dealing.

  • AI tools and academic integrity: AI writing and image tools can explain concepts, suggest outlines and check grammar, but they can also produce invented facts and references, and submitting AI-generated work as your own may break your institution’s rules.
