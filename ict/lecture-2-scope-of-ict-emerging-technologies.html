1. Learning objectives

By the end of this lecture you should be able to:

  1. Describe the use of ICT in five sectors: education, business, governance, healthcare and digital media.
  2. Explain AI, IoT, cloud computing and 5G in simple terms.
  3. Give Pakistani examples of e-governance and online banking.
  4. Evaluate one benefit and one risk of each emerging technology.
  5. Propose an ICT plan for a small local business.

Relevant CLOs: CLO 1 – Understand basic ICT concepts, components and scope.; CLO 2 – Identify different ICT platforms and tools.

2. Introduction

In Lecture 1 we looked inside an ICT system. Now we step back and ask where these systems are used and where they are going. Almost every sector in Pakistan has been reshaped by ICT in the last fifteen years: students submit assignments on Google Classroom, shopkeepers accept Easypaisa, patients book doctors on Marham and citizens verify property records online. At the same time, emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence and the Internet of Things are moving from research labs into ordinary phones and homes. Understanding this scope helps you choose a career, spot opportunities for local businesses and recognise the risks that come with new technology.

Roman Urdu: ICT ka scope bohat wasee hai: taleem, karobar, hukoomat, sehat aur media sab is par chal rahe hain. Naye trends jaise Artificial Intelligence, Internet of Things, cloud aur 5G aane wale saalon mein kaam ka tareeqa badal denge. Is lecture mein hum dekhenge ke yeh technologies Pakistan aur Bhimber mein kahan nazar aati hain.

3. Detailed explanation

3.1 ICT in education

ICT changes how students learn and how institutions manage teaching. Learning Management Systems such as Google Classroom and Moodle distribute material and collect assignments. Video lectures, digital libraries and online courses let a student in Bhimber learn from teachers anywhere in the world. Administration also depends on ICT: admissions, fee challans, attendance and results are increasingly handled online. The Higher Education Commission’s digital library gives university students access to research journals that would otherwise be unaffordable.

Key points
  • Online classes and recorded lectures
  • Learning Management Systems (Classroom, Moodle, Canvas)
  • Digital libraries and e-books
  • Online admissions, fee payment and results

3.2 ICT in business

Businesses use ICT to sell, manage and communicate. Point-of-sale systems record every sale and update stock automatically. Spreadsheets and accounting software track income and expenses. Social media pages and WhatsApp Business let a small shop reach customers without paying for a physical advertisement. E-commerce platforms such as Daraz allow a seller in a small city to deliver across Pakistan. Freelancing platforms have made Pakistan one of the largest sources of online freelancers, with young people earning from graphic design, writing and programming.

Key points
  • E-commerce and online marketplaces
  • Digital marketing on Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp Business
  • Inventory and accounting software
  • Freelancing and remote work

3.3 ICT in governance (e-governance)

E-governance means delivering government services and information through ICT. It reduces queues, paperwork and opportunities for bribery, because each step is recorded. In Pakistan, NADRA issues identity cards through a national database, FBR’s IRIS portal lets citizens file tax returns online, the Pakistan Citizen’s Portal allows complaints to be lodged and tracked from a phone, and many provinces offer online driving licence verification and land record services.

Key points
  • NADRA identity and verification
  • FBR IRIS online tax filing
  • Pakistan Citizen’s Portal for complaints
  • Online land records and licence verification

3.4 ICT in healthcare and digital media

Hospitals use ICT for patient records, digital imaging, laboratory reports and appointment systems. Telemedicine connects patients in remote areas to doctors in big cities by video call. In digital media, news, entertainment and advertising have moved online: people now watch YouTube and stream dramas instead of only waiting for a TV schedule, and anyone with a phone can publish content. This brings opportunities for creators but also spreads misinformation quickly, a topic we return to in Lecture 12.

Key points
  • Electronic health records and digital X-ray
  • Telemedicine and online appointments
  • Online news, streaming and content creation
  • Digital advertising

3.5 Emerging technologies

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is software that performs tasks that normally need human intelligence, such as recognising speech, translating languages or recommending videos. Machine learning, a branch of AI, lets software learn patterns from data instead of following only fixed rules. The Internet of Things (IoT) connects everyday objects such as meters, cameras and water pumps to the internet so they can send data and be controlled remotely. Cloud computing provides computing power, storage and software over the internet on demand, so a small business can rent a server instead of buying one. 5G is the fifth generation of mobile networks, offering much higher speed and lower delay than 4G, which supports IoT devices and real-time video.

Key points
  • AI: chatbots, face unlock, translation, recommendations
  • IoT: smart meters, CCTV, smart irrigation
  • Cloud computing: SaaS (Google Docs), storage, rented servers
  • 5G: higher speed, low latency, many connected devices

4. Technical terminology

E-governance
Delivery of government services and information through ICT.
E-commerce
Buying and selling goods or services over the internet.
Artificial Intelligence
Software that performs tasks needing human-like intelligence.
Machine learning
A type of AI in which systems learn patterns from data.
IoT
Internet of Things: everyday objects connected to the internet.
SaaS
Software as a Service: applications used through a browser on subscription or free.
Latency
The delay between sending and receiving data over a network.
Telemedicine
Providing medical consultation remotely using ICT.

Important definitions

Scope of ICT: The range of fields and activities in which ICT is applied.
Emerging technology: A new technology that is developing quickly and expected to have a large social or economic effect.
Cloud computing: On-demand delivery of computing resources such as storage, servers and software over the internet.

5. Step-by-step example

Worked example: how a recommendation system decides what you watch next

  1. Data collection: the app records which videos you watch, skip, like and how long you watch.
  2. Pattern finding: a machine learning model compares your history with millions of other users.
  3. Prediction: it estimates which unseen videos you are most likely to watch fully.
  4. Ranking: the top predictions appear on your home screen.
  5. Feedback: your next clicks become new data, and the cycle repeats.

6. Pakistan-based examples

Pakistan Citizen’s Portal

Citizens lodge complaints about services through a mobile app; the complaint is routed to the relevant department and the citizen can track its status.

Freelancing

Pakistan is regularly listed among the top countries for freelancers on global platforms. Many students begin freelancing in graphic design, content writing or web development while still studying.

7. Bhimber case study

Case study: taking Bhimber Mart online

Bhimber Mart is a medium-sized general store on the main bazaar. The owner keeps sales in a paper register and loses customers to larger cities. A student proposes an ICT plan: a WhatsApp Business catalogue with prices, a Facebook page for offers, an Excel sheet to track daily sales and stock, Easypaisa and JazzCash QR codes for payment, and home delivery orders taken on WhatsApp. In three months the shop records which items sell fastest and reorders before they run out.

Questions for analysis
  1. Which sectors of ICT scope does this plan use?
  2. What emerging technology could Bhimber Mart use in future, and how?
  3. Name one risk of the new system and one way to reduce it.

8. Real-life applications

  • Smart classrooms and online courses
  • E-commerce stores and digital wallets
  • Online tax filing and complaint portals
  • Telemedicine and digital health records
  • Smart meters, CCTV and agriculture sensors

9. Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Services become faster and available 24 hours a day.
  • Transparency improves because transactions are recorded.
  • New jobs such as freelancing and digital marketing appear.
  • Remote areas can access education and healthcare.

Disadvantages

  • The digital divide leaves people without devices or internet behind.
  • Automation and AI can replace some routine jobs.
  • IoT and cloud systems raise privacy and security concerns.
  • Misinformation spreads quickly on digital media.

12. Important points for examination

  • For scope questions, organise your answer by sector and give one Pakistani example per sector.
  • Define each emerging technology in one line before giving examples.
  • Always include at least one disadvantage; examiners reward balanced answers.
  • Know the expansion of IoT, AI, SaaS and 5G.

13. Lecture summary

  • ICT is used across education, business, governance, health and media.
  • E-governance makes services faster and more transparent.
  • AI learns from data; IoT connects objects; cloud rents computing; 5G speeds up networks.
  • Each technology brings both opportunities and risks.
  • Local businesses can grow using simple, low-cost ICT tools.

14. Short questions

  1. What is e-governance? Give two Pakistani examples.
  2. Define the Internet of Things with an example.
  3. What is the difference between AI and machine learning?
  4. Write two uses of ICT in healthcare.
  5. What is meant by the digital divide?
  6. Expand SaaS and give an example.

Long questions

  1. Discuss the scope of ICT in any four sectors with suitable Pakistani examples.
  2. Explain four emerging technologies and evaluate one advantage and one risk of each.
  3. Prepare an ICT plan to modernise a small shop in your area.

16. Assignment

Choose one sector (education, business, governance, health or media). Write a three-page report on how ICT is used in that sector in Pakistan, including at least three real services or apps, one emerging technology that could improve it, and one risk with a suggested solution. Cite every source.

Homework

List five apps on your phone and write which ICT sector each belongs to and whether it uses AI in any way (for example, recommendations or face unlock).
