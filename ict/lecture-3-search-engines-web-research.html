1. Learning objectives

By the end of this lecture you should be able to:

  1. Explain how a search engine crawls, indexes and ranks web pages.
  2. Write focused keyword queries for academic questions.
  3. Use operators: quotes, minus, site:, filetype:, OR and intitle:.
  4. Evaluate a source using the CRAAP test.
  5. Compare Google and Bing and know when to use specialised search tools such as Google Scholar.

Relevant CLOs: CLO 2 – Identify different ICT platforms and tools.; CLO 3 – Apply ICT tools in everyday, academic and professional activities.

2. Introduction

Most students search the way they speak: they type a full question and click the first result. That works for simple facts, but university assignments need accurate, citable sources. A search engine is not a list of truths; it is a ranking of pages that its software predicts you want. This lecture explains what happens behind the search box, teaches the operators that turn a vague query into a precise one, and gives you a simple test to decide whether a page deserves to be in your assignment.

Roman Urdu: Search engine internet ki library ka catalogue hai. Sahi keywords aur operators (jaise quotes, site:, filetype:) se aap seconds mein sahi jawab dhoond sakte hain. Lekin har website par bharosa nahi kiya ja sakta: source, date aur author check karna zaroori hai.

3. Detailed explanation

3.1 How a search engine works

A search engine does three jobs. Crawling: automated programs called crawlers or spiders follow links from page to page and download their content. Indexing: the content is analysed and stored in a giant index, like the index at the back of a book, that records which words appear on which pages. Ranking: when you type a query, the engine searches its index and orders the matching pages using hundreds of signals such as relevance of words, quality of the site, links from other pages, your location and freshness. Results marked ‘Sponsored’ or ‘Ad’ are paid placements, not necessarily the best answers.

Key points
  • Crawl: discover pages by following links
  • Index: store words and their page locations
  • Rank: order results by relevance and quality signals
  • Ads are labelled ‘Sponsored’ and are paid for

3.2 Choosing keywords

Good searching begins with good keywords. Remove filler words, keep the key concepts and add a word that describes the type of source you need. Instead of ‘what are the effects of mobile phones on students in Pakistan’, search ‘mobile phone use academic performance Pakistan study’. Use specific terms (‘literacy rate’ rather than ‘education level’), try synonyms when results are poor and add a year for recent data. Searching is iterative: read the first results, learn the correct terminology they use, then refine your query.

Key points
  • Identify 2–4 main concepts
  • Remove question words and filler
  • Add type words: study, report, statistics, pdf
  • Refine using terms found in good results

3.3 Search operators

Operators are symbols or words that give the search engine exact instructions. Quotation marks search for an exact phrase. A minus sign excludes a word. site: limits results to one website or domain type, such as site:gov.pk for Pakistani government pages or site:edu.pk for universities. filetype: finds a specific format such as PDF or PPTX. OR (in capitals) finds either term. intitle: requires a word in the page title. Operators can be combined: “literacy rate” site:pbs.gov.pk filetype:pdf.

Key points
  • “exact phrase”
  • jaguar -car (exclude)
  • site:gov.pk or site:hec.gov.pk
  • filetype:pdf / filetype:pptx
  • Matric OR SSC
  • intitle:syllabus

3.4 Evaluating sources: the CRAAP test

Before using a source, check five things. Currency: when was it published or updated, and is that recent enough for your topic? Relevance: does it answer your question at the right level? Authority: who wrote it, and what are their qualifications or organisation? Accuracy: is it supported by evidence, data or references, and do other reliable sources agree? Purpose: is it written to inform, or to sell, persuade or entertain? Official domains such as .gov.pk and .edu.pk, peer-reviewed journals and recognised news organisations are usually more reliable than anonymous blogs, forums or social media posts.

Key points
  • Currency – date
  • Relevance – fit to your question
  • Authority – author and organisation
  • Accuracy – evidence and references
  • Purpose – inform, sell or persuade?

3.5 Google, Bing and specialised tools

Google has the largest share of searches in Pakistan and strong local results. Bing, built into Microsoft Edge and Windows, offers similar operators, image search and AI-assisted answers through Copilot. Results differ because each engine has its own index and ranking. For academic work, use Google Scholar to find research papers and see how many times they are cited, the HEC Digital Library for journal access through your university, and official statistics sites such as the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics. AI chat tools can summarise information but may produce confident mistakes, so always verify facts in an original source.

Key points
  • Google: largest index, strong local results
  • Bing: built into Edge and Windows
  • Google Scholar: research papers and citations
  • Official sources for statistics
  • AI answers must be verified

4. Technical terminology

Crawler
A program that automatically visits web pages by following links.
Index
The search engine’s database of words and the pages that contain them.
Query
The words typed into a search engine.
Search operator
A symbol or word that refines a search, such as quotes or site:.
Domain
The address of a website, such as hec.gov.pk.
Citation
A reference that tells readers where information came from.
Peer review
Checking of research by other experts before publication.
SERP
Search Engine Results Page.

Important definitions

Search engine: A software system that finds and ranks web pages that match a user’s query.
Web research: The systematic process of finding, evaluating and using information from the internet.
Reliable source: A source that is current, written by a qualified author, supported by evidence and published for an informative purpose.

5. Step-by-step example

Worked example: finding Pakistan’s official literacy rate

  1. Start broad: literacy rate Pakistan. Notice many news sites with different numbers.
  2. Identify the official source mentioned in results: the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS).
  3. Narrow the search: literacy rate site:pbs.gov.pk.
  4. Find the report format: literacy rate site:pbs.gov.pk filetype:pdf.
  5. Open the report, find the table and note the survey year.
  6. Cite it: Pakistan Bureau of Statistics, report title, year, URL.

6. Pakistan-based examples

Checking a university’s recognition

Students can verify whether a university is recognised by searching site:hec.gov.pk recognised universities instead of trusting an advertisement.

Scholarship research

Searching “scholarship” site:hec.gov.pk filetype:pdf finds official scholarship notices and avoids fake scholarship pages that ask for fees.

7. Bhimber case study

Case study: researching tourism in Bhimber district

A student must write a report on tourist sites in Bhimber district, including Baghsar Fort and Baghsar Lake. A first search returns travel blogs with conflicting history. The student refines the search with “Baghsar Fort” history, then adds site:gov.pk and site:edu.pk to find official tourism and university pages, and uses Google Scholar for any published research. She records each source in a table with its date, author and purpose, and drops two blogs that copy text from each other without references.

Questions for analysis
  1. Which operators did the student use, and why?
  2. Apply the CRAAP test to a travel blog with no author name.
  3. Why should the student record the date of each source?

8. Real-life applications

  • Writing assignments and research papers
  • Checking news before sharing it
  • Finding official forms and notices
  • Comparing product prices and reviews
  • Finding past papers and syllabus documents

9. Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Instant access to millions of documents
  • Operators make searches precise and fast
  • Free access to official data and research summaries
  • Search history helps return to useful sources

Disadvantages

  • Results may be biased by ranking and advertising
  • Much online content is inaccurate or outdated
  • Information overload wastes time
  • Search engines collect data about users

12. Important points for examination

  • Be able to explain crawl, index and rank in order.
  • Write operators exactly, including the colon (site: and filetype:).
  • CRAAP: remember all five letters and what each checks.
  • Give an academic example when asked about effective searching.

13. Lecture summary

  • Search engines crawl, index and rank pages.
  • Effective searches use focused keywords, not full questions.
  • Operators such as quotes, site: and filetype: narrow results.
  • Use the CRAAP test before trusting a source.
  • Use Google Scholar and official sites for academic work and verify AI answers.

14. Short questions

  1. What are the three main functions of a search engine?
  2. Write the operator to search only Pakistani government sites.
  3. What is the purpose of quotation marks in a search?
  4. List the five parts of the CRAAP test.
  5. What is the difference between a sponsored result and an organic result?
  6. Name two search engines and one academic search tool.

Long questions

  1. Explain how a search engine works from crawling to displaying results.
  2. Describe six search operators with examples, and show how they can be combined for academic research.
  3. How can a student evaluate whether a website is reliable? Explain with the CRAAP test and an example.

16. Assignment

Research question: ‘What is the current state of internet access in Azad Jammu and Kashmir?’ Find five sources using at least four different operators. For each source, write the query used, the URL, the date and a CRAAP evaluation. Conclude with a 300-word summary using only the sources you rated reliable.

Homework

Search the same question on Google and Bing. Compare the first five results of each and write three differences you notice.

17. Suggested classroom discussion

  2. Why do different search engines show different results for the same query?
