Out-of-School Children in Pakistan: What the Data Says

Pakistan has made progress in percentage terms, but millions of children are still not in school.

Home  /  Blog  /  Out-of-School Children in Pakistan: What the Data Says

By Startwell Education · 2026-11-13

The numbers

The Pakistan Education Statistics 2021–22 report estimates 26.2 million out-of-school children, or 39% of school-age children. The share fell from 44% in 2016–17, but the number rose from 22.02 million to 26.21 million because the population grew faster.

By level

  • Primary: 36% out of school (10.77 million children)
  • Middle: 30%
  • High: 44%
  • Higher secondary: 60%

Poverty makes the biggest difference

The report’s chart, based on 2018 household survey data, shows 51% of primary-age children from the poorest households out of school, against 4% from the richest.

Distance matters too

UNESCO’s 2026 report cites a Pakistan study finding that girls aged 10 to 12 who travel 45 to 60 minutes to school are 15 percentage points less likely to attend than girls living within 15 minutes.

The world context

Globally, 273 million were out of school in 2024: 79 million of primary age, 64 million of lower secondary age and 130 million of upper secondary age. The number has risen for seven years in a row, but UNESCO notes some countries have cut out-of-school rates by 80% or more since 2000, so progress is possible.

What families and schools can do

  • Enrol children early and keep them through transitions such as primary to middle.
  • Choose a school within a safe, reasonable distance.
  • Ask schools about support for children who are behind.
